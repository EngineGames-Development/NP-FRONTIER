## Definitions (recalled)

- **B2:** the full binary basis, i.e. fan-in-2 circuits whose gates may compute any of the 16 two-input Boolean functions. Size = number of gates.
- **Affine disperser for dimension d:** f: F_2^n → {0,1} that is non-constant on every affine subspace of F_2^n of dimension ≥ d.
- Explicit (polynomial-time computable) affine dispersers for sublinear dimension d = o(n) are known, so the result is a lower bound for an explicit function in P. The specific constructions are not recorded here (unverified).

## Relevant theorem

**Theorem 1.1** (theorem number as reported in the WebSearch summary of the ECCC text). Any B2 circuit computing an affine disperser for sublinear dimension has size at least **3.1n − o(n)**.

**Method:**
- Gate elimination with affine restrictions, i.e. substitutions x_i := affine function of other variables.
- A more involved case analysis than Find–Golovnev–Hirsch–Kulikov 2016 (`find-2016-better-than-3n.md`), which reduces the "bottleneck" structures that arise during elimination.
- The authors describe this as a study of the local topology of a minimal circuit.

## What it rules out or limits

This is a frontier result, not a no-go theorem. It calibrates what is known:
- As of the refereed literature checked here, the best lower bound on general B2 circuit size for any explicit function (in P, hence also in NP) is **linear**, about 3.1n.
- A claimed lower bound of the form ω(n) for an explicit NP function over B2, let alone superpolynomial, would be a major advance. It must be audited with this gap in mind.
- An unrefereed 2026 claim of 3.5n − o(n) exists (`mishra-2026-3.5n-lower-bound.md`).

## Why it matters for NP-FRONTIER

- Calibration: P ≠ NP via circuits needs NP ⊄ P/poly, i.e. superpolynomial size. Current techniques give 3.1n (or a claimed 3.5n).
- Gate elimination, the only technique behind these bounds, provably cannot reach large linear constants with a bounded number of substitutions per step (`golovnev-2016-gate-elimination-limits.md`).
- Any lower-bound hypothesis for general circuits must use a different technique.

## Limitations

- Applies to the B2 basis only. Recalled, unverified: other models have different frontiers, e.g. about 5n for the De Morgan basis U2, and n^{3−o(1)} for De Morgan formulas.
- The hard function is in P, so the result says nothing about P vs NP specifically. It is about explicit circuit lower bounds.
